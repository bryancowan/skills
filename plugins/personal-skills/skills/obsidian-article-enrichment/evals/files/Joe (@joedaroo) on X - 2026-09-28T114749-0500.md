Speaking for myself, not my employer (I work on agent security at OpenAI).

A lot of the commentary on the recent incidents gets it wrong. "Just sandbox it" sounds easy, but RL environments need real tools, network access, and compute that changes constantly, and thousands of researchers modify them across tens of thousands of parallel runs. ==Every change can quietly break an earlier security assumption.==

What helps: lock down access from first principles and red-team environments before scaling training; treat alignment and independent security controls as complements; monitor model actions with evidence the model can't touch, and make sure someone can halt a run and revoke access. Prefer VM isolation like Firecracker or Kata over shared-kernel containers.

==The biggest gap is cultural. Safety researchers often lack incident-response experience; security people often lack ML context.== Put both in the room. Build a culture of reasonable paranoia, keep the people who keep finding holes, and distrust anyone who says their system is perfectly safe.
